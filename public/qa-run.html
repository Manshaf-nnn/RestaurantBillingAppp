Before you start — read this

  1. Register your own restaurant. Go to /register and sign up with your own email and a restaurant name that is obviously a test, e.g. QA — Ravi. You will land on a page saying Waiting for approval.
  2. Ask for approval. A new account cannot open the dashboard until an administrator approves it. Message whoever sent you this link and wait — it takes them a few seconds. Press Refresh status until it lets you in.
  3. Never test on anyone else's restaurant. Everything you create — dishes, staff, orders, payments — must be inside your own test account.
  4. Use a real browser and open the console. Press F12 → Console tab, and keep it open. Red errors there are bugs even when the screen looks fine. Test M3 asks about this.

Sign in once and stay signed in — logging in more than 8 times in five minutes locks the account for a while, by design.

How to mark a test

Pass — it did what the “Expect” line says. Fail — it did something else. Say what happened, in the notes: what you clicked, what you expected, what you actually got, and the exact wording of any error. Blocked — you could not run it (something earlier is broken, or you lack access).

A note on a Fail is what makes it fixable. Compare these two: “discount doesn't work” versus “On the POS, a 10% discount on a 1,000 bill took the total to 950 instead of 900 — it looks like the tax is being discounted too. Order #260908-004.” The second one gets fixed today.

Always include the order or invoice number, the branch you were on, and the time. If the screen shows an error with a long code (a “digest”), copy it exactly.

How severe is it?

  • Critical — money is wrong, data is lost, or nobody can work: a bill that adds up wrong, stock that does not move, a page nobody can open, one restaurant seeing another's data.
  • Major — a feature does not work but there is a way round it: an export that fails, a filter that ignores you, a button that does nothing.
  • Minor — wrong label, awkward layout, missing hint, an odd but harmless number format.

Put the word Critical, Major or Minor at the start of your note.
